# 06 — Systems, Verbs & Combat

## The verb set (what the player actually does)

Ranked by how much of the game they occupy:

| Verb | Description | Share |
|------|-------------|-------|
| **Speak / choose** | Sourced dialogue choices, testimony, negotiation. | High |
| **Move / position** | Navigate spaces; where you stand carries meaning. | High |
| **Observe / gaze** | Attention-driven interaction; reading a scene. | High |
| **Build / organize** | Raise the mosque, pair the brotherhood, distribute aid, dig the trench. | Medium |
| **Endure / hold** | Withstand persecution, siege, stillness. | Medium |
| **Restrain / forgive** | The deliberate non-violence mechanic at key beats. | Low count, high weight |
| **Fight (era-accurate)** | Defensive melee/archery in 3 battle missions. | Low |

## Non-combat systems

- **Dialogue & testimony.** Branching, sourced choices; persuasion by sincerity
  and evidence, not intimidation. Tracks trust with key characters.
- **Community-building.** Light management layers in M12 (brotherhood pairings,
  covenant terms) and M5 (rationing during the boycott) — humane, low-twitch.
- **Stealth/hold.** Cave of Thawr, ʿAlī's decoy — tension via concealment and
  nerve, with no requirement to kill.
- **Codex.** Optional historical entries unlock as you encounter people,
  places, and events — the educational backbone, kept opt-in so it never
  lectures.

## Combat design (the minority — Badr, Uḥud, Khandaq)

Combat exists because these defensive battles are historical fact, but it is
designed to **avoid glorifying killing**:

- **Era-accurate only.** Swords, spears, bows, shields, cavalry. No firearms,
  no anachronisms (R3).
- **Defensive framing.** Objectives are *protect, hold the line, survive,
  endure the siege* — never "achieve a kill count." The community's survival,
  not enemy body count, is the win condition.
- **Restrained presentation.** Stylized, non-gory hits; the human cost is
  shown as *loss and grief* (Ḥamza at Uḥud), not as a power fantasy.
- **Mercy outcomes preserved (R7).** The historical aftermaths are playable
  and encouraged: ransoming and educating captives after Badr; the general
  amnesty at the opening of Mecca. Enemies are a historical faction, never a
  religion/ethnicity to be "cleansed."
- **Consequence-driven.** Uḥud is deliberately *losable through indiscipline*,
  teaching obedience to a plan and the reality of consequence rather than
  rewarding lone heroics.

### Combat mechanics sketch

- Grounded, weighty melee (block / strike / shove) — readable and deliberate,
  not a combo-spectacle.
- Bow sections (Uḥud ridge) emphasizing **holding an assigned position**.
- Formation and morale matter more than individual prowess; you fight *with*
  and *for* the community, reinforcing collective over individual.

## Difficulty

Two intertwined axes:

- **Mechanical** (standard combat/stealth difficulty, fully adjustable; combat
  can be eased for players there for the story).
- **Moral/emotional** (the weight of the choices) — *not* dialed down; this is
  the intended core of the experience, with comfort options (pace/skip) rather
  than removal.

## Accessibility

Subtitles and narration are first-class (the game is narration-driven by
design). Colorblind-safe UI, remappable controls, adjustable text size,
reduced-motion options for the light-heavy sacred sequences, and content
guidance toggles.
