# 02 — Historical Research (Seerah)

This is the source material the missions draw from — a structured summary of
the life of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. Dates are CE and approximate where scholars
differ. **This is a design reference, not a substitute for scholarly review.**
Primary traditional sources include Ibn Hisham's recension of Ibn Ishaq's
*Sīrah*, al-Wāqidī, al-Ṭabarī, and the canonical hadith collections; modern
syntheses include Martin Lings' *Muhammad: His Life Based on the Earliest
Sources* and Safi-ur-Rahman al-Mubarakpuri's *The Sealed Nectar* (Ar-Raheeq
Al-Makhtum).

> Production note: before any release, every mission's historical claims must
> be checked against these sources by a qualified reviewer (see R8).

## A. The Meccan period (before prophethood)

- **c. 570 — Year of the Elephant.** Born in Mecca, Banu Hashim clan of
  Quraysh. Father ʿAbdullāh died before his birth; mother Āmina died when he
  was ~6. Raised by grandfather ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, then uncle Abū Ṭālib.
- **Childhood & youth.** Time with the wet-nurse Ḥalīma; worked as a shepherd;
  joined trade caravans to Syria. Earned the epithets **al-Amīn** (the
  Trustworthy) and **aṣ-Ṣādiq** (the Truthful) for his integrity.
- **c. 595 — Marriage to Khadīja bint Khuwaylid**, a respected merchant who
  employed and then proposed to him. A partnership of deep mutual love; she
  becomes his first supporter at the moment of revelation.
- **c. 605 — Rebuilding of the Kaʿba.** After a flood damaged it, the clans
  quarreled over who would have the honor of replacing the Black Stone
  (al-Ḥajar al-Aswad). They agreed to accept the judgment of the next man to
  enter — it was Muhammad ﷺ. He placed the stone on his cloak and had a leader
  of each clan lift a corner together, then set it himself: a resolution of
  conflict through shared honor. **(Prototype mission.)**

## B. Revelation & early Islam (610–615)

- **610 — First revelation, Cave of Ḥirāʾ.** In retreat on Jabal an-Nūr, the
  angel Jibrīl commands *"Iqraʾ"* ("Read/Recite"). The first verses of Sūrat
  al-ʿAlaq. He returns home trembling; Khadīja comforts and believes him.
- **The first believers.** Khadīja; the young ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib; the freedman
  Zayd ibn Ḥāritha; the merchant Abū Bakr — who in turn brings others
  (ʿUthmān, az-Zubayr, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf, Saʿd, Ṭalḥa).
- **Three years of private invitation**, then the public call from the hill of
  aṣ-Ṣafā, met with mockery and hostility — especially from Abū Lahab and Abū
  Jahl.

## C. Persecution (615–619)

- **Persecution of the vulnerable.** Slaves and the weak who believed were
  tortured. **Bilāl ibn Rabāḥ** is pinned under a rock in the sun, saying only
  *"Aḥad, Aḥad"* (One, One); Abū Bakr buys his freedom. The family of **Yāsir**
  is tortured; **Sumayya** is killed — the first martyr of Islam.
- **First Hijra to Abyssinia (615).** A group migrates to the Christian king
  the **Najāshī (Negus)**, who, after hearing Jaʿfar recite Qurʾān about Jesus
  and Mary, refuses Quraysh's demand to extradite them. A landmark of refuge
  and interfaith respect.
- **The boycott (c. 616–619).** Quraysh impose a social and economic boycott
  on Banū Hāshim, confining them to a valley in hunger for ~3 years until the
  pact document is found eaten by termites except the name of God.

## D. The Year of Sorrow & turning outward (619–621)

- **619 — ʿĀm al-Ḥuzn (Year of Sorrow).** Both **Abū Ṭālib** (his protector)
  and **Khadīja** (his beloved support) die within a short span. A chapter of
  grief and vulnerability.
- **The journey to Ṭāʾif.** Seeking new support, he is rejected and stoned by
  the town's youth until bloodied. The famous moment: offered the power to
  destroy the town, he prays instead for their descendants' guidance. **A
  defining lesson in mercy over revenge.**
- **The Night Journey (al-Isrāʾ wa-l-Miʿrāj).** A miraculous spiritual journey
  to Jerusalem and the heavens; the obligation of the five daily prayers is
  established. (Handled abstractly — R5.)
- **The Pledges of al-ʿAqaba (621–622).** Pilgrims from Yathrib (later Medina)
  accept Islam and pledge to protect him — opening the door to the Hijra.

## E. The Hijra (622)

- **Migration to Medina.** With assassination plotted, **ʿAlī sleeps in the
  Prophet's bed** as a decoy; the Prophet and **Abū Bakr** hide in the **Cave
  of Thawr** while searchers stand at its mouth (the tradition of the spider's
  web and nesting dove). They reach Yathrib, renamed **al-Madīna**.
- **Foundations.** Building the Prophet's Mosque; the **brotherhood**
  (muʾākhāh) pairing the Meccan emigrants (Muhājirūn) with the Medinan helpers
  (Anṣār); the **Constitution of Medina**, a pluralistic pact including the
  city's Jewish tribes.

## F. The Medinan defensive battles (the combat minority)

- **624 — Badr.** ~313 Muslims against ~1,000 Quraysh; an against-the-odds
  victory that secures the community. **(Combat mission.)**
- **625 — Uḥud.** A reversal: archers leave their assigned post to collect
  spoils, the Muslims are flanked, and **Ḥamza**, the Prophet's uncle, is
  martyred. A hard lesson in discipline and obedience to a plan. **(Combat +
  consequence mission.)**
- **627 — al-Khandaq (the Trench).** On the advice of **Salmān al-Fārisī**,
  the Muslims dig a trench to neutralize a large besieging coalition; the siege
  fails. **(Strategy/defense mission.)**

## G. Diplomacy, return, and farewell (628–632)

- **628 — Treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyya.** A pilgrimage turned into a negotiated
  truce whose terms seem unfavorable but prove a strategic and moral victory —
  patience and the long view over immediate gratification.
- **Letters to rulers.** Invitations sent to Heraclius, the Negus, the ruler
  of Egypt, and others — Islam reaching beyond Arabia by word.
- **630 — The opening of Mecca (Fatḥ Makka).** The Prophet enters his
  birthplace largely without bloodshed and declares a general **amnesty** to
  the very people who had persecuted and exiled the Muslims: *"Go, for you are
  free."* The Kaʿba is cleared of idols. **The climactic mission of mercy.**
- **632 — The Farewell Pilgrimage & Sermon.** On ʿArafa he delivers the
  Farewell Sermon — equality of all people, sanctity of life and property,
  rights, and the end of tribal blood-feuds.
- **632 — His passing** in Medina. The community's grief; Abū Bakr's address:
  *"Whoever worshipped Muhammad — Muhammad is dead. Whoever worshipped God —
  God is living and never dies."*

## Recurring themes to surface in design

- Truthfulness and trust (**al-Amīn**) as the foundation of his authority.
- Patience (**ṣabr**) under persecution rather than retaliation.
- Mercy and forgiveness at the moment of greatest power (Ṭāʾif, Fatḥ Makka).
- Justice and dignity across class and tribe (Bilāl, the Constitution, the
  Farewell Sermon).
- Brotherhood and refuge (Abyssinia, the Anṣār).
- Consultation and humility (Salmān's trench, learning from Uḥud).
