# 01 — Vision & Design Principles

## Vision statement

> A first-person, historically faithful journey through the life of Prophet
> Muhammad ﷺ that makes the player *feel* what the early Muslim community felt
> — the loneliness of conviction, the cost of patience, the weight of
> leadership, and the radical power of mercy — rendered in realistic,
> present-day-quality 3D.

The player does not "win" by defeating people. They progress by *holding to
principle under pressure*: telling the truth when it is dangerous, showing
restraint when revenge is easy, forgiving when they have the power to punish.

## The "Counter-Strike" question, answered

The user's reference to Counter-Strike is about **graphical fidelity** —
realistic, immersive 3D worlds — not about gunplay or deathmatch. We honor the
*visual* ambition (see `07-art-direction.md`) and explicitly reject the
*mechanical* one. The result is closer in spirit to narrative immersive sims
(e.g. the historical immersion of *Assassin's Creed*'s recreated cities, or
the moral-weight design of *This War of Mine*) than to a competitive shooter.

## Six design pillars

1. **Reverence first.** Every system is checked against the red lines in the
   README. If a mechanic would trivialize, depict, or distort the sacred, it
   is cut — no exceptions.
2. **Empathy through perspective.** First-person presence is the core tool.
   You don't read about persecution; you stand inside it.
3. **Conviction over conquest.** The dominant verbs are *speak, witness,
   choose, protect, endure, forgive* — not *kill*.
4. **Historical truth.** Missions are built from the Seerah and authenticated
   sources (`02-historical-research.md`). Dramatization is allowed; invention
   of the Prophet's words or fabrication of events is not.
5. **Psychological weight.** The game is engineered to leave the player
   *changed* — see `05-psychological-design.md`. Difficulty is emotional and
   moral, not just mechanical.
6. **Inclusive framing.** Designed to be meaningful for Muslims and
   respectfully educational for non-Muslims, without polemic.

## The respectful-design rulebook (operational)

These are concrete rules content/engineering must follow:

| # | Rule | Why |
|---|------|-----|
| R1 | No model, face, body, shadow, or reflection of the Prophet ﷺ. | Mainstream Islamic prohibition on depiction. |
| R2 | No mirror/water-reflection surfaces in player-controlled scenes that would render an avatar. | Prevents accidental self-depiction. |
| R3 | His speech only as on-screen narration sourced from tradition; never improvised VO. | Avoids putting invented words in his mouth. |
| R4 | The four "Rightly Guided" companions and other Sahaba *may* be depicted respectfully (named, with honorifics). | They are widely depicted in Islamic media; still handled with dignity. |
| R5 | Revelation, angels, and the Night Journey are conveyed via light/sound/abstraction — never a literal creature model or gamified sequence. | Reverence; avoids depicting the unseen (ghayb). |
| R6 | No collectible/score framing on sacred acts (prayer, revelation). Worship is never a "minigame for points." | Prevents trivialization. |
| R7 | Enemy combatants in battles are historical factions, not a religion or ethnicity to be "exterminated." Always allow the historical mercy outcomes. | Avoids hate-framing; reflects the actual history of amnesty. |
| R8 | A scholarly review gate sits before any public release. | Accuracy + community trust. |

## Tone

Solemn, warm, and human — not grim. The early community knew real joy,
brotherhood, and humor alongside hardship. The game should breathe: quiet
domestic moments with Khadijah, the relief of arriving in Medina, the laughter
of the Ansar, set against the heavier chapters.
